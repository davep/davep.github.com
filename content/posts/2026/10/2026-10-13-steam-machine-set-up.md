A couple of days ago, thanks to some free time on a Sunday afternoon, I got
round to setting up my [newly acquired Steam
Machine](/2026/10/08/steam-machine-and-steam-controller.html). While I don't
have a lot to say about it, I thought I'd note here that the event happened
and jot some notes about how it went.

On unboxing, two things struck me: just how small it is, while also how
hefty it feels. I'm not a hardware person, as I've mentioned here many times
before, so can't speak to the how and why -- but it feels like the whole
thing is cleverly and densely packed. Having owned plenty of PCs over the
years, especially a lot of towers and mini towers, it was pretty neat to
hold in my hands a machine that would put most of them to shame.

I also really appreciate that it can pretty much hide behind a plant pot
next to the TV. It's just... there. It's not a *feature*.

As for setting up, that was dead simple. I pulled the machine and cable out
of the box, unboxed the controller that came with it too, plugged an
Ethernet cable between it and my router, fired it up and followed the
instructions. Logging in was the usual dead-simple QR code scan from the
[Steam](/tag/steam/) app on my phone.

The *only* issue I ran into was when setting up the [Steam
Controller](/tag/steam-controller/) itself. While it worked perfectly with
its own puck plugged in, right from the start (so I could do the setup
without need for a keyboard and mouse), things didn't go so well when it
offered to switch over to the internal puck. When it asked if I wanted to do
that, the `Pair` button (I think that's what it was called) could be pressed
but always resulted in an instant error. Then using `Retry` was an instant
error too. I tried a few times, to no success, so then just carried on using
the controller's own puck on the end of a cable.

After going through that, it *then* wanted to do updates on the puck and
controller, which I followed. I had hoped that at that point it might repeat
the process to move to the internal puck but that wasn't offered as an
option.

Once through all that I was all up and running and my library was available.
I tried a couple of games and the whole thing felt nice and fast. In fact,
it felt like exactly what I wanted: a better-performing [Steam
Deck](/tag/steam-deck/) that is always plugged into my TV.

After testing a couple of games, I searched online and then dived into the
settings and found that, from there, I could swap the controller over to the
internal puck. The method for doing this wasn't obviously available in the
settings, hence the search online. Annoyingly I can't find the page I landed
on, but I seem to remember it simply involved directly plugging the
controller into the machine (USB->USB, *not* connected to the puck) and then
messing in the controller settings on the machine.

Overall, while there was that little hiccup, the experience pretty much felt
like setting up a console -- which seems ideal to me. As for what I can do
with it: the "Great on Machine" tab in my library looks to contain about a
fifth of my total library, and a lot of that is made up of games I'm happy
to play. I've not paid too much attention to the what and why of the other
four-fifths -- I also imagine there are a good few in there that would also
work but which haven't had the seal of approval yet.

Would I recommend this to anyone? Eh, if you're a full-on "PC master race"
type who loves to build their own kit and cares about the hardware and
stuff... I would imagine not. On the other hand, if you're someone who has a
reasonable library on Steam and wants a low-to-no-maintenance PC that's
almost invisible in your living room: I'd say it's ideal. Sure, the price
isn't great, but that's the way stuff is at the moment[^1]. Additionally,
you're getting a desktop Linux machine *and* something that can be tweaked
and tailored how you like, if that's your thing.

Next up on my list of things to do with it, aside from playing on it more
(currently low-key obsessed with [Iron
Nest](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2950790/IRON_NEST_Heavy_Turret_Simulator/)
and am also enjoying having more runs in [Vampire
Survivors](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1794680/Vampire_Survivors/)),
is to figure out the easiest and best way to record video (with audio and
voice) and, ideally, also [stream to
YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@DavePearson/streams). Given the nature of
the machine (Linux box with gaming UI) I imagine it's as "simple"[^2] as
adding OBS and setting things up. To be fair to the consoles though: this is
where they *do* win just a little; on both my Xbox Series S and my PS5 there
are dedicated tools for streaming. I wish the Steam Machine came with
similar built-in functionality so I could be extra lazy and also have a
setup that is very unlikely to be disturbed by random updates.

[^1]: Thanks AI, I guess.

[^2]: As in: likely doable with some tinkering and work.
