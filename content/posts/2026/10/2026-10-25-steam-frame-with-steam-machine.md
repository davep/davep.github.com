Back when the whole new Steam hardware ecosystem was announced, I remember
there being a fair bit of talk about how one of the benefits of having a
[Steam Frame](/tag/steam-frame/) *and* a [Steam
Machine](/tag/steam-machine/) would be that you could either play some
[VR](/tag/vr/) games standalone, or when you wanted to play something a bit
more demanding, you could stream from your Steam Machine. While a bit
expensive if you were creating your setup from scratch, the idea of a
[PCVR](/tag/pcvr/) gaming setup that is all based around one ecosystem was
very much appealing.

Fast-forward to the drip-feed availability of the Machine and then later the
Frame, we ended up with a pair of devices that actually didn't really work
with each other... yet. Reddit was, of course, mad at Valve. Personally,
while I did end up with both devices, I was okay with this as it was obvious
that work was under way to make this a reality and that it wasn't too far
off.

Then, yesterday morning, I noticed that [SteamOS
3.9](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1675200/view/694275728822244963?l=english)
was released. This entry in the release notes caught my eye:

> Improved support for Steam Frame wireless adapter

I had about 90 minutes to kill, so I thought I'd give things a go. I updated
the OS on the Machine and updated the Frame too. After that, I "unboxed" (it
was still in its little paper jacket) the wireless adaptor and plugged it
in, and the Machine recognised it right away. At this point, though, it got
a little muddled. I wish I'd kept notes of what I was doing, but from this
point on I sort of had to make things up as I went along. What I *was*
expecting was for SteamOS, after I'd paired the Frame (something it prompted
me to do), to do anything and everything else to get me playing a game,
streamed to the Frame, seamlessly. That part didn't happen. There was no
prompt to install SteamVR, it wasn't even clear if I needed to or should. I
did anyway just in case (and that took a little bit of faff; I seem to
remember that a straightforward search of the store for SteamVR actually
didn't show it -- but I forget how I found it in the end).

At this point I tried to follow my nose to see if I could get anything
working. My intention was to try out [Gunman
Contracts](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2421750/Gunman_Contracts_Stand_Alone/),
which had been *very* disappointing as a Frame-native experience. Would it
run better with the Machine doing the work? Here's what happened when I
tried:

1. First time I ended up with the game running on the Machine, displaying
   flat on my TV, and with the Frame saying it had timed out.
2. Second time I ended up with the game running in 2D/flat mode *in* the
   Frame.
3. Third time I finally got it running on the Machine, but as a VR game
   streamed to the Frame.

I can't remember the steps I took to get there (again: I only had a short
amount of time and hadn't thought to make notes as I went), but once I had
this going things seemed to work well. As for the performance of Gunman
Contracts itself: eh... it was visibly better than native on the Frame, but
it wasn't great. I did some tweaking to the settings and eventually got it
running in a way that felt pretty responsive.

I feel it's a game that needs a *lot* more work to make it run comfortably
on the Machine, let alone native on the Frame.

One thing I did seem to notice was that it's really tricky to pull up the
game's menu while you're playing it. It *seems* like you're supposed to
press down on the d-pad, maybe. But I found that sometimes it was just a
press, other times it was a press and hold for a second or so, yet other
times nothing worked. I'm left wondering if it's sensitive to your location
in the game. I need to do more experimentation there.

Getting low on time, and battery on the Frame, I decided I had to try
another game that I really want to be able to play more of again: [VTOL
VR](https://store.steampowered.com/app/667970/VTOL_VR/). That installed and
ran up just fine. In no time whatsoever I was up and in the air.

![VTOL VR running](/attachments/2026/10/25/vtol-vr.webp#centre)

Things did get a little confused at this point though. In attempting to take
this screenshot, I accidentally managed to do the SteamVR "recentre my view"
gesture (press and hold the Steam button on the controller). This resulted
in me being sat on top of the canopy and turned through 90 degrees. Now, I
seem to remember that there is a way to recentre yourself in the VTOL VR
cockpit, but I couldn't remember the button combination off-hand. I also
seem to remember there's a way to do it via an MFD menu but, given I was sat
on top of the canopy, I couldn't reach that.

So, in conclusion: it *does* seem that we're now at a point where the Steam
Machine and Steam Frame will talk to each other, and you can play games on
the Frame where the Machine is doing the heavy lifting. This is good news.
On the other hand it does seem like there's a lot of tidying up to be done
with this new VR ecosystem, and I suspect that plenty of games need to take
the new Frame controllers into account too.

When I next get a chance I'm going to go through this process again, and try
and keep notes of what I do and what the results are.
