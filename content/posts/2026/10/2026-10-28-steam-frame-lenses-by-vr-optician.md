Back on Monday, I finally received some lens inserts for my [Steam
Frame](/tag/steam-frame/). I originally registered my interest in getting
some back on September 20th, got an email to say they were available for
purchase on October 1st, and ordered them the same day. They finally turned
up on the 26th (two days ago). Considering that the Steam Frame is still a
pretty new device, and I imagine the company were still getting fully set up
to handle all of this, this felt like a good turnaround.

![The lenses in their bags](/attachments/2026/10/28/lenses_bagged.webp#centre)

The company I used this time around (I've used others for my Quest 2 and
PSVR2 headsets) was [VR Optician](https://vroptician.com/). In my case I was
ordering [actual prescription
lenses](https://vroptician.com/prescription-lens-inserts/valve-steam-frame-vrchat).
While I am mildly short-sighted, and also have to contend with astigmatism,
I can mostly get away with using a headset without correction. It's not
ideal, but it's also not impossible to play and read in the headset. In
fact, for the last few weeks that I've had the Steam Frame, I've got used to
it.

On Monday evening I took a moment to fit the lenses and give them a very
quick try-out. Oh, the difference! It's not a surprise, of course, but I was
already impressed with how clear the Frame is; with the lenses in place it
felt like a whole new headset again.

I ordered the "slim/comfort" versions, as opposed to the "advanced
eye-tracking/VRChat" versions[^1]. I don't need advanced eye-tracking (at
least for the foreseeable future) and I'm *very* unlikely to ever need to be
doing anything in VRChat. Despite being the "slim" version, they obviously
add some extra bulk to the lenses.

![A single lens](/attachments/2026/10/28/single.webp#centre)

I can't say I'm currently noticing a difference in how they feel with
respect to my eyes (although this week isn't one that's giving me much
opportunity to mess about in [VR](/tag/vr/)), but there is a noticeable
extra bit of "pinch" on the sides of my nose. Hopefully, with a little more
time in the headset, I'll find a sweet spot when it comes to comfort.

As for whether it was worth the £69.00 plus shipping... yeah, for sure. They
correct my eyesight, let me get the most out of the headset, and they let me
do it in a way that protects the actual Frame's lenses (both in terms of
giving them a less-expensive cover *and* by removing the urge to try and
wear glasses inside the headset).

[^1]: If you're curious why there are two different versions and what the
    difference is: [the page](https://vroptician.com/prescription-lens-inserts/valve-steam-frame-vrchat)
    has a pop-out that goes into detail.
