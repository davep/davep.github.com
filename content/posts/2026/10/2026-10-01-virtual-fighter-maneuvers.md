I realise I've been a bit quiet on the blog front for a few days, in part
because I've also been a bit quiet on the coding front too. Looking at my
GitHub contribution graph, the previous three days where I didn't commit
anything are the longest run of no-commit days I've had in about 10 months.
There are a couple of reasons for this: the first is that I've reached a bit
of a natural pause point with [my work on Rogallo](/tag/rogallo/) (there's a
biggish update brewing, but I've been taking my time with it), the second is
-- as I expected -- [getting my hands on the Steam
Frame](/2026/09/23/steam-frame-is-here.html).

I haven't been playing with it exclusively -- when I have a good amount of
free time I am [back to
streaming](https://www.youtube.com/@DavePearson/streams) -- but I have been
dipping in on occasion, and some of those occasions would be moments where
I'd reach for the [MacBook](/tag/macbook-air/) and bang out some code.

I'm still not set up for full-on [PCVR](/tag/pcvr/), but I have "discovered"
a game that runs really well *on* the Steam Frame, and it's one that's
related to [a favourite of mine in
VR](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLUY2FpMDyORCEqHuAxNrL0WV6GV4RFzZv):
[Virtual Fighter
Maneuvers](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3952640/Virtual_Fighter_Maneuvers/).

Simply put: it's a *very* cut-down version of VTOL VR, designed to run well
on standalone headsets. Graphically it's simplistic, and from a gameplay
point of view it's *very* focused. There's no single-player campaign.
There's no multiplayer campaign. There's no story at all. You strap on your
headset, fire up the game, jump into a lobby and off you go: dogfight time!

I love it for that. I've always loved flight sims, and they're even more fun
in VR. I like the slow pace and the sense of immersion. But if you're going
to fly in VR it takes a bit of time to get set up and plan things. This is
the total opposite. I love that I can dive in for 30 minutes, have a fun
experience, and then be done. It's ideal for quick, casual sessions.

That's not to say I *only* want to play for those short bursts -- I have
played longer sessions -- it's more to point out that it's a game that
combines perfectly with the low-activation-energy accessibility of the Steam
Frame. It's a game that makes the Frame feel as easy to pick up as the
[Steam Deck](/tag/steam-deck/).

Of course, being a mainly multiplayer game, your chances of having a good
time are mixed[^1]. I've been into a couple of lobbies that have been really
fun, with a good mix of folk, who all seem to have joined to have a good
time. I've also joined matches where it's just kids wanting to be edgelords
on the Internet. Thankfully, so far, it's more the former than the latter.
Sadly, though, there don't seem to be that many people playing it. In my (UK
timezone) evenings there's normally, at most, four matches on the go that
you might be able to join.

There also seems to be a 1v1 matchmaking service so you can get a ranking by
battling folk at a similar level to you (I'm a newb to the game, of course);
I've yet to actually get such a match going.

This one is a keeper and I can see myself diving into it often for a good
while to come.

[^1]: It does have bot matches too, so you can do some limited single-player gameplay.
