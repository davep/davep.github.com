Since getting into [Gemini](/tag/gemini-protocol/), one of my daily rituals
has been to tend to my plant in
[AstroBotany](gemini://astrobotany.mozz.us/). This morning, when trying to
visit there with [Rogallo](https://rogallo.davep.dev/), I was greeted with
this:

![Expired certificate](/attachments/2026/10/26/astro-error.webp#centre)

In other words: the certificate has expired[^1]. At this point, the error
dialog isn't *that* useful. It's pretty much designed for dealing with
capsules that are using self-signed certificates -- where TOFU is being used
and so it would normally make sense to just forget and re-establish trust.
With an expired CA certificate, the act of forgetting does nothing, it'll
just fail again.

While it's technically fine to simply refuse to progress, in this case, it's
not exactly friendly to the user. What would be sensible would be to allow
an *"Ignore"* option. This would, I imagine, ignore the problem for the
current Rogallo session -- ideally with some sort of indication that the
capsule is suspicious at the moment. Also, the dialog that gives you this
option should show all the certificate details so the user can make an
informed decision.

The issue here is that, until now, Wasat has dealt with security-type
problems by raising a single `SecurityError` exception. Aside from
dumpster-diving the description in the exception, there's no way of knowing
the nature of the security issue.
[v1.10.0](https://wasat.davep.dev/changelog/#v1100) addresses this by adding
and raising many different exceptions:

- `CertificateExpiredError`
- `CertificateNotYetValidError`
- `CertificateRejectedError`
- `CertificateRevokedError`
- `CertificateUntrustedError`
- `FingerprintMismatchError`
- `HostnameMismatchError`

All of these inherit from `SecurityError` to maintain
backward-compatibility.

While this change doesn't alter the way that Rogallo handles this situation
right now, it does at least set me up for [tackling the work I have
planned](https://github.com/davep/rogallo/issues/498).

[^1]: By the looks of things it expired at 2026-10-26T01:52:07+00:00
