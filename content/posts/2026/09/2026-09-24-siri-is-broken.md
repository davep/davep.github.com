I think, in part, I regret upgrading any of my [Apple](/tag/apple/) devices
to the 27 line of operating systems. While my experience as a whole has been
pretty favourable, the one thing I was looking forward to is not just
disappointing, but is a huge step backwards.

![Waiting for Siri to do anything](/attachments/2026/09/24/siri-waiting.webp#centre)

I don't use [Siri](/tag/siri/) for many things, but I do use it a lot for a
small number of core things. On any given day I'll lift my watch up and
speak to it to set a timer or a reminder. Occasionally I'll ask other
things, but they're the main uses. Thanks to the fairly recent addition of
on-device voice handling, these requests have been near-instant.

With the new Siri that comes with iOS 27, etc., if I'm really lucky the
reply will be super slow (getting on for 20 seconds or so to do the job),
but more often than not it just doesn't work at all. Either it tells me that
my main device isn't available right now[^1] (which I take to mean my
[iPhone](/tag/iphone/), which is wild given it's always in my pocket at the
time), or it just busy spins for ages and then simply times out and does
nothing.

If I try to use Siri on the iPhone itself, it either just busy waits for
ages without getting anything done, or it finally tells me it isn't working.

![Siri failing](/attachments/2026/09/24/siri-failed.webp#centre)

I was one of the people who upgraded to the 16 Pro because of the promise of
*"Apple Intelligence"*. I was, unsurprisingly, kind of annoyed that they
didn't deliver. But it was fine, the phone is a good one and it's still
serving me well. Also, if I'm honest, I was comfortable with the idea that
they chose to not really implement anything[^2] rather than implement
something bad.

Sadly though, based on my experience so far, they've implemented something
bad. Since upgrading, on the Watch and iPhone, I've found that it's either
horrifically slow or it just doesn't work. On top of that, I've lost all the
benefit of the on-device voice handling of my watches.

[^1]: Or words to that effect; I can't remember the exact wording.

[^2]: I mean, of course, they did do the image playground and writing tools,
    but they're hardly the leap forward in assistant intelligence that was
    originally being hinted at.
