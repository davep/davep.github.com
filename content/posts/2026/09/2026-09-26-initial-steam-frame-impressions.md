Yesterday evening I finally had the chance to unbox my [Steam
Frame](/tag/steam-frame/) and have an initial play with it. Here are my
impressions of it after playing for a couple of hours. Note that this
*isn't* intended to be any kind of review, just some jotted notes about
things I noticed and concluded. The order might appear a little random too.

## Unboxing

The first thing I noticed was the obvious lack of any actual instructions.
Either I missed the obvious, or there was no leaflet and nothing on the box
to tell me how to get it up and running. I had already read online what to
do so didn't have any issues, but this still strikes me as odd. I guess the
Frame isn't intended for the full consumer market and so the kind of
enthusiast who plays with SteamVR is likely curious enough to go find
information or work things out, but it still feels like an odd omission.

The other thing that feels like a serious omission is the complete lack of
any sort of welcome experience. I remember when I first got my Quest 2, the
introduction app was a whole thing and did a great job of showing off the
hardware. Heck, even the [Steam Deck](/tag/steam-deck/) has [Aperture Desk
Job](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1902490/Aperture_Desk_Job/). It
seems like a very odd choice that something new for the Frame wasn't
created.

For example, [The Lab](https://store.steampowered.com/app/450390/The_Lab/)
is a decade old now; it seems strange to me that they didn't refresh it and
promote it as a standalone initial experience for the Frame -- perhaps using
it to show off some of the new capabilities or something.

## Comfort

While I did buy the ergonomic kit, I didn't set any of that up right away. I
was keen to try the device out so my initial test was without the top strap
in place. While I've since fitted it, and find it a useful addition, I'm
amazed at just how comfortable the headset is to wear. I've spent many
hundreds of hours wearing the Quest 2 and the PSVR2, neither of which was
horrible, but both of them felt uncomfortable after some use. Based on my
initial impressions the Frame feels like something that could be worn for
pretty long sessions without any problem.

To be clear: The Steam Frame is by far the most comfortable headset I've
worn. This in turn makes it feel like the headset with the least activation
energy required when it comes to being motivated to "jump into VR".

## Controllers

The controllers are... okay, I guess. Compared to the headset they feel
cheap, which they might well be. Based on my experience with the Quest 2,
the controllers are the things that take the most punishment and so they're
most likely to be the things that need replacing most often. Perhaps
they're designed with the right balance of *"last long enough"* and
*"affordable to replace"*.

The main issue I have with the controllers is they seem to have crammed too
much into them. They've put all four buttons on the right controller, and a
D-pad on the left controller. Perhaps I'm missing something here but this
feels like overkill. I found that this made the controllers feel far too
busy. All of this also means that the two top buttons (the ones normally
used to pull up menus of some description) feel just out of reach. During my
time playing last night I found I was having to constantly readjust my grip
to reach different buttons, which then meant I was often having to peek
under the headset to actually see where my fingers were landing.

Honestly, in terms of ergonomics, I'd say the Frame's controllers are worse
than those of the Quest 2, but slightly better than the ones that come with
the PSVR2[^1].

There's also the complication of the controller straps that come in the
ergonomic kit. I love these sorts of straps (the kind that goes around the
back of your hand) as they generally make controller use more comfortable.
It's great to not *have* to keep gripping the controller all the time, and
it also feels more natural if you need to throw something in VR. But it
really feels like whoever designed the controller layout never spoke with
whoever designed the straps. The combination ends up being pretty awkward.

I've never used the [Index
controllers](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1059550/Valve_Index_Controllers/),
but everyone I've spoken to who has always seems to sing their praises.
Based on that, the Frame's controllers feel like they can't have come from
the same company.

## Inside the headset

I've read a good few comments from people on Reddit that say that the
display in the headset is pretty dark. That wasn't my experience at all. Out
of the box I found the display bright enough, and that wasn't even turned up
as high as it will go.

The other thing I found was that the display felt like the clearest one I've
tried out yet. I wear glasses; I'm short-sighted and have an astigmatism
that needs correcting. Given the design of the headset there's no way I was
going to wear my specs while using it, so for the moment I'm playing
unaided[^2]. Despite that I found the display very readable (to be fair, I'm
not *that* short-sighted).

What is slightly disappointing is the lack of an actual "home" when you boot
up the headset. This feels like a missed opportunity. Like, sure, the
floating menus and the stark landscape are fine and do the job, but the
actual home to exist in always felt like a fun thing. Even the Quest 2 has
this -- surely it could be done on the Frame?

## Audio

I really like the audio. My VR audio experience comes from the stock Quest 2
and also stock PSVR2. The Frame is obviously very similar to the Quest 2, in
that the speakers are "near" your ears but don't cover them in any way. I
like this. I like this a lot. I find this a really comfortable way to
experience VR (and, while it's not an issue at the moment, the ideal setup
for recording your voice for video-making or streaming purposes -- you're
less likely to end up shouting). I've never liked the PSVR2 in-ear buds so
I'm glad it's nothing like that.

While playing, I found that the sense of direction of sounds was pretty spot
on, which is something that very obviously matters to immersion. That level
of immersion, combined with an awareness of your own surroundings, feels
like just the right balance.

## Battery life

The battery life is not great, it has to be said. I think I got something
like 40 minutes out of the device when I first booted it up. In all
fairness, I can't remember what level it was on to start with, and I was
also doing an OS upgrade, controller update, and the installation of a game
or two.

The second part of my initial session started with something like 95%
battery available. That second session ended up on about 20% battery left
after playing Alyx for the better part of an hour, give or take (I didn't
make a point of actually timing it).

I feel like, with a full charge, and depending on what I do, I might get
around 90 minutes out of the device. This feels less than I'd get out of the
Quest 2 back in the day.

All of this was, of course, playing a game locally on the headset itself. It
will be interesting to see how it compares when in PC-based PCVR streaming
mode using the supplied dongle.

Given my interest in using this mostly in standalone mode, I can see myself
buying an external power unit and hooking it up while it's stuck in a pocket
or something.

## Ergonomic kit

The ergonomic kit feels like a thing that should have been included in the
price, if I'm honest. The additional strap for the headset is, as I
mentioned above, nice to have and improves the comfort from comfortable to
very comfortable. The straps for the controllers feel a little cheap but do
the job.

I've not tried out the nose guard thing yet. There's no doubt that a *lot*
of light leaks around the nose, but at the same time that's actually quite
handy for quickly glancing at the world around you if you need to be able to
check on something (your phone, or where your fingers are on the controller
because of the issues I mention above).

Now, this might just be me being a bit dense: I found the instructions that
came with the kit kind of confusing when it came to the controller straps. I
got there in the end, but I feel like the visuals printed on the box could
have been *so* much better.

Thankfully my kit was complete. I've seen reports of people finding broken
parts, missing parts, or things like two right-handed controller straps
rather than a left and a right.

## Gaming

For my initial session I only played games standalone. While I do have a PC
for PCVR purposes, it's still packed away after my move last year. I will
drag it out and give it a go at some point, but the standalone gaming
abilities are what interest me most at the moment.

### Gunman Contracts - Stand Alone

The first game I decided to try out was [Gunman Contracts - Stand
Alone](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2421750/Gunman_Contracts__Stand_Alone/).
While the *"Stand Alone"* in the name is in reference to it no longer being
a mod for Half-Life: Alyx, my understanding is that it is intended to be
playable standalone on the Steam Frame.

It isn't. It so very isn't.

The FPS was horrible, and I noticed lots of graphical distortion as I tried
to play. I never made it out of the home bunker as the whole thing was just
a laggy mess.

This doesn't surprise me though. Based on other reviews it looks like it's
not the best experience even when playing in full PCVR mode. I've also
played it flat-screen on the Steam Deck and it's okay there, but obviously
needs some work.

I will revisit this, as I noticed in the settings for it that everything
seemed to be turned up to the max. Textures were on *Ultra* for example,
which seems like a wild choice out of the box for a game running on a
standalone VR headset. Given this I'm going to assume the game makes zero
effort to detect the environment it's running in and to configure good
defaults.

### Gun Club VR

Next up I tried [Gun Club
VR](https://store.steampowered.com/app/691320/Gun_Club_VR/). This is a game
I've played before native to the Quest 2, and also in PCVR. Given it's an
older game I had high hopes for it. I was right to. This was pin-sharp and
buttery smooth. In terms of how it looked and felt in the headset it was
night and day compared to Gunman Contracts.

Sure, I'm comparing apples and oranges there in terms of game age and what's
being asked of the hardware, but I think it's a good comparison of the
different ends of that spectrum.

While Gunman Contracts left me concerned that a lot of VR gaming would be
off-limits on the Frame, Gun Club VR gave me hope that many of the classics
will run well (perhaps after some tweaks by the authors).

### Half-Life: Alyx

The third and final game I tried out was Half-Life: Alyx. I've [played this
in full PCVR mode
before](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLUY2FpMDyORAiNuu3K9NXzAVAgUJs-0sA),
and do feel it's pretty much the peak VR experience. The idea that this
could work standalone was wild to me. I know Valve have worked hard on
making it work, and are promoting it to the point of bundling it with the
Frame[^3], but it still felt like it was going to be a bad time.

It wasn't a bad time. It was a good time.

While it's very obvious that the graphics have been dialled down, and while
I did see a fair bit of pop-in of assets from time to time, generally it
looked good and performed well. In my peripheral vision I could obviously
see that the resolution was super-low, but no matter where I was directly
looking, I saw a quality image (presumably eye-tracked foveated rendering at
work, although I've not checked if it is used in Alyx). There was almost no
stutter, no lag to speak of, and it felt as good as I remember when playing
it in full PCVR mode.

## Conclusion

Overall my first experience is pretty positive. Out of the box the Steam
Frame is the most comfortable VR experience I've had yet. Given I'm writing
this just one week after getting the purchase invitation email, I have high
hopes for this device. It seems obvious that there's a load of software and
firmware improvements to come, and I can imagine a good third-party market
of add-on products to augment the use of the headset (mics, headphones, care
and storage, power without full tether, etc.). I also imagine there will be
a scramble to optimise a good number of VR games and experiences to get them
working well standalone.

It is, without question, expensive for what it is. I very much doubt that
this was Valve's intention or doing. The demand for various types of
hardware is having a broad impact and I don't need to detail that. Taking
this fact into account, this feels like a great little product that's only
going to improve with time.

While I'm not sure I'll return to recording VR videos again, at least not
with the frequency I used to, I *will* be returning to enjoying playing VR
again thanks to this little device making it very accessible.

[^1]: I'd place the PSVR2 higher than the Frame if it wasn't for the fact
    that I find the grips of the controllers far too bulky and very
    difficult to keep gripped for an extended period of time.

[^2]: I will be getting prescription inserts when they become available.

[^3]: Pity us poor souls who already own it I guess.
